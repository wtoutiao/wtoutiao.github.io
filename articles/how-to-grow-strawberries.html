How to Grow Strawberries

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Nothing beats the taste of a sun-warmed strawberry you grew yourself. Strawberries are surprisingly easy to grow — in the ground, in pots, or even in hanging baskets.

Choose Your Type

June-bearing varieties give one big harvest in early summer. Day-neutral and everbearing types produce smaller crops over the whole season — great for containers and a long supply.

Planting

Plant strawberries in full sun with rich, well-drained soil. Space plants about 12 inches apart. In containers, use a pot at least 8 inches deep with good drainage. Set the crown at soil level — not buried.

Watering and Feeding

Keep soil evenly moist, especially while fruit is forming. Water at the base to keep berries dry. Feed with a balanced fertilizer at planting and a potassium-rich feed as flowers appear.

Mulch and Cleanliness

Mulch with straw to keep berries off the soil and reduce weeds. This also keeps the fruit clean and less prone to rot.

Harvesting

Pick berries when fully red and fragrant. Harvest every couple of days during the season. Pinch off runners (long stems) to keep the plant focused on fruiting.

Protecting From Pests

Birds love berries — cover plants with netting when fruit starts to ripen. Keep the bed clean to deter slugs and rot.
