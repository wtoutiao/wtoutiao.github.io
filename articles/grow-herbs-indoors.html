10 Easy Herbs to Grow Indoors Year-Round

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Fresh herbs make every meal better — and you don't need a garden to grow them. With a sunny windowsill and a few pots, you can have fresh basil, mint, and rosemary all year long. Here are the 10 easiest herbs for indoor growing.

The 10 Easiest Indoor Herbs

  1. Basil — loves warmth and bright light; pinch flowers to keep leaves coming.
  2. Mint — nearly impossible to kill; give it its own pot because it spreads fast.
  3. Rosemary — prefers drier soil and lots of sun; great for roasting.
  4. Parsley — slow to start but reliable; harvest outer leaves first.
  5. Chives — cut-and-come-again; grows from seeds or small bulbs.
  6. Thyme — small and tidy; needs well-draining soil.
  7. Oregano — hardy and forgiving; tolerates some neglect.
  8. Cilantro (Coriander) — quick but short-lived; sow new seeds every few weeks.
  9. Sage — drought-tolerant; give it bright light and good drainage.
  10. Lemon Balm — cheerful and easy; related to mint and just as tough.

What You Need to Get Started

  • Light — a south or west-facing window with 6+ hours of sun is ideal.
  • Containers — any pot with drainage holes; 4–6 inches deep is plenty.
  • Soil — a well-draining potting mix, not garden soil.
  • Water — keep the top of the soil slightly moist, but never soggy.

Caring for Your Indoor Herbs

Rotate pots weekly so all sides get light. Harvest regularly — cutting herbs encourages bushier growth. If your window is too dark in winter, a simple LED grow light fixes the problem cheaply.
