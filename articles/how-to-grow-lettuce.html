How to Grow Lettuce in Containers

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Lettuce is one of the fastest and most rewarding crops for small spaces. You can be harvesting fresh salad greens in as little as three to four weeks — even from a windowsill pot.

Choose Fast Varieties

Look for loose-leaf and cut-and-come-again types like Lollo Rosso, Oak Leaf, and Buttercrunch. They regrow after cutting, giving you multiple harvests from one planting.

Containers and Soil

Use a shallow container at least 6 inches deep with drainage holes. Fill with a light potting mix. Lettuce has shallow roots and does best in cool, moist conditions.

Sowing

Sow seeds thinly on the surface, cover with a thin layer of soil, and keep moist. Thin seedlings to about 4 inches apart. In hot weather, provide afternoon shade to prevent bolting.

Watering

Lettuce needs consistent moisture — never let the soil dry out completely. Water gently in the morning. A saucer under the pot helps on hot days.

Harvesting

Harvest by cutting outer leaves about an inch above the base. The plant keeps producing new leaves for weeks. Sow a new batch every couple of weeks for a continuous supply.
