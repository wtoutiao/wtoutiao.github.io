How to Compost at Home: A Simple Beginner's Guide

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Composting turns your kitchen scraps and garden waste into free, nutrient-rich fertilizer — and it's much easier than most people think. Here's how to start composting at home without fuss.

Choose Your Composting Method

  • Backyard pile or bin — the classic, best for gardens.
  • Tumbler — easy to turn, faster results, tidy.
  • Worm bin (vermicomposting) — perfect for apartments and balconies.

What to Add

Balance two kinds of material: greens (nitrogen) like fruit peels, veggie scraps, coffee grounds, and fresh grass; and browns (carbon) like dry leaves, cardboard, and straw. Aim for roughly two parts browns to one part greens by volume.

What to Avoid

Skip meat, dairy, oily food, and diseased plants — they attract pests and smell. Also avoid pet waste and anything treated with pesticides.

Keep It Working

Moisture should feel like a wrung-out sponge. Turn the pile every week or two to add oxygen. Chop materials smaller for faster breakdown. In 2-4 months you'll have dark, crumbly compost.

Use Your Compost

Mix finished compost into garden beds or pots, or use it as a mulch. It improves soil structure, holds moisture, and feeds your plants naturally.
