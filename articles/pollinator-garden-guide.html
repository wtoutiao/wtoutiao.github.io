How to Start a Pollinator Garden

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

A pollinator garden is one of the most rewarding things you can plant. It brings your yard alive with bees, butterflies, and birds — and it's one of the biggest garden trends right now. Here's how to start one, even in a small space.

Why Pollinators Matter

About one in three bites of food depends on pollinators. By planting for them, you're not just making a beautiful garden — you're supporting your local ecosystem and improving your own vegetable harvest at the same time.

Choose Pollinator-Friendly Plants

Native, nectar-rich plants are the best choice because local pollinators recognize them. Reliable favorites include:

  • Wild bergamot and bee balm — loved by bees and hummingbirds
  • Butterfly milkweed — essential for monarch butterflies
  • Purple coneflower (echinacea) — a pollinator magnet
  • Lavender, salvia, and ornamental thyme — fragrant and bee-friendly
  • Black-eyed Susan and goldenrod — late-season nectar

Plant for Continuous Bloom

Choose plants that flower from early spring to late fall, so pollinators always find food. Aim for at least three plants blooming in every season.

Provide Water and Shelter

Add a shallow water source, like a birdbath with stones, and leave some bare soil and brush piles for nesting bees. Skip the pesticides — they harm the pollinators you're trying to attract.

Start Small

Even a few pots on a balcony count. Plant a mix of natives, skip chemicals, and keep it blooming — that's a pollinator garden.
