How to Water Plants Correctly

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

More plants die from bad watering than any other cause. Learning to water correctly will transform your garden. Here's the simple system that works.

Water Deeply, Less Often

Instead of a little water every day, water deeply a few times a week. This encourages roots to grow down, making plants more drought-tolerant and healthier.

When to Water

Morning is best — water reaches the roots before the sun evaporates it, and leaves dry quickly, reducing disease. Avoid evening watering, which leaves plants wet overnight.

How to Check

Push a finger into the soil about an inch deep. If it feels dry, it's time to water. If it's still moist, wait. This beats any schedule.

Water at the Base

Water the soil around the plant's base, not the leaves. Wet foliage invites fungal diseases. Use a watering can spout or drip irrigation for best results.

Container Plants Differ

Pots dry out much faster than garden beds — check them daily in summer. Self-watering planters or saucers can help during heat waves.

Mulch to Save Water

A 2-3 inch layer of mulch (straw, bark, or leaves) slows evaporation, keeps soil cool, and suppresses weeds — cutting your watering in half.
