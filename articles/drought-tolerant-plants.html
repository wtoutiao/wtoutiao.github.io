Drought-Tolerant Plants: A Water-Smart Garden Guide

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Dry summers and water restrictions are a reality for many gardeners. The smart fix isn't fighting the drought — it's choosing plants that thrive with little water. Here's how to build a garden that stays beautiful even in dry conditions.

Why Go Drought-Tolerant

Drought-tolerant plants are adapted to survive long dry spells with minimal watering. They need less time, less money, and less water — and they stay healthy when thirsty neighbors wilt.

Best Drought-Tolerant Vegetables

  • Tomatoes — deep roots handle dry spells once established
  • Peppers — actually love hot, dry conditions
  • Squash and zucchini — vigorous and drought-hardy
  • Okra — thrives in heat and drought
  • Sweet potatoes — tough and productive

Best Drought-Tolerant Herbs

Rosemary, thyme, oregano, sage, and lavender all love dry, sunny conditions. Mediterranean herbs are nature's drought specialists — the drier, the more fragrant they get.

Water-Smart Techniques

Mulch heavily to lock in moisture. Water deeply and less often to encourage deep roots. Use drip irrigation or watering cans at the base rather than sprinklers. Water early morning so less evaporates.

Improve Your Soil

Rich, organic soil holds water far better than sandy or compacted soil. See our soil improvement guide and composting guide to build soil that retains moisture through dry spells.

Want more crops that handle heat? Try tomatoes and peppers.
