Seed Saving 101: How to Save Seeds From Your Garden

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Saving seeds is one of the most rewarding gardening skills. It's free, it preserves the exact varieties you love, and year after year your plants adapt to your own soil and climate. Here's how to get started.

Why Save Seeds

  • Save money — no more buying seed packets every year
  • Preserve favorites — keep the exact variety you love
  • Adaptation — your seeds gradually become better suited to your garden
  • Self-sufficiency — a resilient supply that doesn't depend on stores

Easiest Seeds for Beginners

Start with self-pollinating crops that are simple to save: beans, peas, lettuce, tomatoes, and peppers. These produce reliable seeds without complicated cross-pollination concerns.

How to Harvest Seeds

Let the fruit fully mature on the plant — longer than you'd eat it. For dry seeds (beans, lettuce, peppers), let pods and seed heads dry completely, then collect the seeds. For wet seeds (tomatoes), scoop the seeds, rinse, and dry them on a paper towel.

Dry and Store

Dry seeds thoroughly in a cool, airy spot for a week or two. Store them in labeled paper envelopes in a cool, dry, dark place. Well-stored seeds can last several years.

Grow and Repeat

Each year, save seeds from your best plants — the ones with the best yield, flavor, and health. Over time, you're breeding plants perfect for your garden.
